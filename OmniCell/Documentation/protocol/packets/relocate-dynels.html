Extracted-client RelocateDynelsIIR_t has vtable 0x1015E4C4, reader 0x1003A61D, writer 0x1003A4EA, dispatcher 0x1003A577 and constructor 0x1003A64B; the reader and the writer emit the same two fields in the same order, and the writer's list helper at 0x1003A73A puts out the X3F1 count and then one Identity per entry, which is the same shape the reader's shared 0x1002BA77 takes. What the three identities are - the message's own, the one in the body, and the ones in the list - was the part left open, and the dispatcher answers it, because the casts it asks for are the names. The message's own identity is resolved as a SimpleChar_t; the body identity with the plain GetDynel, so it is only ever asked to be an n3Dynel_t; each listed identity as a SimpleItem_t. Then it does two things per listed item: n3Dynel_t::RelocateDynel on the body dynel with the item, the null position and the null rotation - so the item becomes a child of the body dynel, sitting at its origin - and at 0x10058903 it hands the same item to the character's inventory with a slot of -1, meaning anywhere. An item that was standing in the world is now hanging off something and listed in someone's inventory, which is what picking something up looks like from the outside. No capture contains one, so RelocateDynelsMatchesTheLayoutTakenFromTheClient pins the layout rather than checking it against retail; before this the message had no C# at all.
