Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: Every byte is identified, and two things this entry used to say were wrong until nine retail copies turned up on 2026-09-11. Extracted-client PlaySoundIIR_c has vtable 0x1015E49C, reader 0x1003A368, writer 0x1003A2F7 and dispatcher 0x1003A262. Two fields: a counted string and an Identity. The count includes the terminator, which this entry denied - it said the string was terminated only in client memory. The writer settles it: at 0x1003A2FE it takes the string's length, adds one, refuses the message unless that minus one is at most 0x3E7, writes the incremented count as an int32 and then writes that many bytes out of the string's own storage, so the NUL travels and is counted. The reader agrees - it refuses a count above 1000 or at or below zero, allocates one byte more than the count and NUL-terminates after what it read, which is a second terminator and is why the one on the wire does no harm. The captures show it plainly: "SM_Sandy_AH_Helpful_Colonist" is twenty eight characters and the count reads twenty nine. Every one of the nine copies failed the round trip on that one byte until it was fixed. The other correction is the consumer. The dispatcher emits GlobalSignals_c + 0x190 with the string and the Identity, and nothing in any shipped image subscribes to it - a signal scan finds the emit and no connect, and a raw scan for the offset across every module finds only unrelated uses. So the client reads this message and drops it, and this entry's claim that the dispatcher hands both to the sound system was not something anyone had checked. What the Identity is comes from the wire rather than from the client, and nine copies are enough to say it. All nine carry identity type 0xC350 with instances between 0x0E930C6E and 0x0E930C82, and every one of those eight distinct instances is also the subject of a SimpleCharFullUpdate in the same session - most of them of a SetWantedDirection and a CharacterAction as well. They are live character dynels. The names line up with them one for one: SM_Sandy_AH_Helpful_Colonist, SM_Sandy_AH_Bored_Traveller, SM_Sandy_AH_Young_Woman, SM_Sandy_AH_Old_Geezer, SM_Sandy_AH_Young_Man, SM_Sandy_AH_Middle_Aged_Guy, SM_Sandy_AH_Curious_Colonist and SM_Sandy_AH_Omni_Tek_Officer - one voice line per NPC type in the newcomer area. So the Identity is the dynel the sound belongs to. Green here means both fields are identified, not that the client does anything with them; it does not.
