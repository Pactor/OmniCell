All 18,530 captured copies now round-trip byte for byte, and SimpleCharFullUpdateNpcMatchesRetailCaptureByteForByte builds a 244-byte quest NPC from nothing and reproduces the retail bytes exactly. Nine fields were missing before that transcription, and the one that mattered was inside the active-nano list: each entry is five int32s, not four. The fifth was being taken for the array header that follows it, which was then not a multiple of 0x3F1 and was silently read as an empty array, so every character with a nano running on it lost the rest of its packet without raising an error. The earlier standing guess about HasExtendedTextures - that it gated four bytes at offset 193 - was wrong in kind: it gates a counted array of forty four byte entries. Two later corrections, both from widening the audit to every capture on disk rather than the recent ones. The bit at 0x08 next to HasHeadMesh was being forced on whenever a head mesh was written, on the strength of a count over 2,331 copies in which HasHeadMesh never appeared without it; over 18,530 copies it appears without it 944 times, so the writer was inventing a byte the sender had not sent, and the bit is now carried through untouched like the other undecided ones. And the four flags that choose a narrow or a wide form for Level, Health, HealthDamage and RunSpeedBase are the sender's to choose, not a function of the value: fifteen copies put a level of ten or twenty in a short and ten of those put a health of under a hundred in a full int. A writer that picks the width from the value makes those packets a byte or four short and everything after the level wrong. SimpleCharFullUpdateMessage.WireWidths records the four bits as they arrived and the writer honours them; null, which is every message the server originates, means derive as before.

The layout is as solid as it ever was - 27,468 copies round trip byte for byte and the reader is walked - but green here means a byte has a known meaning, and eleven of them do not. A pass over the dispatcher on 2026-09-11 named four fields and confirmed two more, all by the same shape - push the value, push the stat id, call the setter at [character + 0xE8] vtable + 0x40. The byte behind flag 0x20000000 is shadowbreed, 532. The int32 inside the second flag word's first block, which the model called Unknown1, is mechdata, 662 - the same stat MechInfo carries, named by the same route. The two bytes behind 0x800000 and 0x1000000 are monster scale, 360, written into two different maps on the skill subsystem rather than into the stat table. And the pair the model already called AccountFlags and Expansions is confirmed rather than corrected: the client reads one int32 and splits it, low half to stat 389 expansion and high half to stat 660 accountflags, which is exactly the two int16s in the order this model reads them. Two preconditions came out of reading the dispatcher's opening on 2026-09-11 and neither was written down anywhere. It resolves the message's own Identity with n3Dynel_t::GetDynel and gives up if that succeeds - the test at 0x10078392 bails when the dynel already exists - so this message creates a character and will not update one that is already there. And it passes the member at + 0x20 to n3Playfield_t::GetPlayfield and gives up if that returns nothing, which makes + 0x20 the playfield id and makes a copy naming a playfield the client has not loaded a no-op. A server that sends one of these for a character the client can already see is sending it into the ground. The target Identity behind flag 0x20 is resolved with GetDynel too, immediately after, which is consistent with the HasFightingTarget name the flag already carries - a fighting target has to be something that exists. And the list behind 0x40000000, which no capture contains, does have a consumer after all: the dispatcher walks it at 0x10078A8F and hands each entry's two int32s to 0x1004B515 on the subsystem at [character + 0x1B8]. One more thing the round trip cannot see, found the same way. The health damage field has three arms and they do not carry the same quantity. With HasSmallHealthDamage the client reads a byte at 0x100794D5 and stores health minus it at 0x100794E5; with HasSmallHealth instead it reads an int16 and stores it as it stands; with neither, an int32, also as it stands. So the byte form is a delta and the other two are values, and a server that means to say damage of N must send health minus N when it sets that flag and N when it does not. Reading and writing the raw byte round trips either way, which is why 24,903 copies never showed it. The field is left raw deliberately - converting it would make the round trip depend on Health being read correctly first. Sweeping the reader for arithmetic between a read and a store finds one more, and finding it needed the sweep widened - the first pass looked for sub, add, imul and shl and missed a mask. The packed appearance word is split into six fields at 0x1007921B and after: bits 0 to 2, then 3 to 4, 5 to 7, 8 to 9, 10 to 11 and 12 to 16. This model had five, and its Race was value >> 10 - everything above bit 9 rather than the two bits the client takes. The dispatcher settles it: + 0x89 holds (value >> 10) & 3 and goes to stat 89, race, at 0x10078549, while bits 12 to 16 go to + 0x90 and nothing in the dispatcher reads them back. Race is now two bits and the five above it are their own field, which matters on 230 captured copies where that upper field is not zero - on those the old Race reported a number the client never uses. The packed word is one uint32 on the wire and round tripped through the mistake, which is why nothing objected. The small forms - Family, LosHeight, Level, RunSpeed - really are plain widenings. MonsterData is confirmed rather than changed on the same pass: the message + 0xA4 goes into stat 0x167, 359, monsterdata, and only when it is not zero. The last row closed on 2026-09-12, and it closed somewhere none of the earlier passes had looked: the client's resource database rather than its code. The weapon pair's fourth int32 is the four-character code of the record that supplied the weapon, and the third is the key it is filed under. They are equal in nine of the twelve captured entries because for an equipped monster weapon those are the same thing; the three that differ are martial arts, dimach and brawl, whose keys are the stat ids 100, 144 and 142.
