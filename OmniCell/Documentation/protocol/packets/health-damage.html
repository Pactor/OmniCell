Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: Every byte is identified. Extracted-client HealthDamageIIR_t has vtable 0x10167B88 and reader 0x100A0576, which reads four int32s, an Identity and one more int32 with no version in front of any of it. The apply at 0x100A0611 refuses the message unless the message identity is a CanbeAffected and then uses every field. The first int32 goes straight into stat 0x1B, health, so it is what the character has left rather than what it lost. The second is signed and the branch at 0x100A0664 is on its sign: negative is damage, anything else a heal, and either way the client displays the magnitude. The fourth, when non-zero, sends the dispatcher into 0x1005B3D8, which switches on it to pick one of five Feedback_DeathBy... lines and then sets health to zero, so it is the cause of death and zero means nobody died. The Identity is not the target - the message identity is the character whose health changed - but the other party, resolved separately and handed to the feedback line, so it is the attacker or the healer. The last two took a second pass and the client settles both. The third int32 is a damage type in the client's own numbering: the formatter looks it up in a table at 0x10036CF5 and prints "Missing damagetype: %d" when it is not there, and the table is built in one run at 0x10033C41 - projectile 90, melee 91, energy 92, chemical 93, radiation 94, cold 95, poison 96, fire 97, nano 168, unknown 27, fall 474, backstab 489. Eight of those ids are the armour class stat ids, which is why four values landing in 90 to 96 looked like armour classes before the table was found; the other four show it is a numbering of its own. Zero is not in the table at all - the formatter substitutes 27, unknown, at 0x10012C87 - and the damage arm tests for 474, fall, before anything else, because falling has its own line. The trailing int32 is half an identity and the client supplies the other half. The formatter tests it against zero at 0x10012C97 and, when it is set, builds an Identity with the type hard-coded to 1000020 and this as the instance, hands it to the item manager's find-or-create at 0x10082EE2 - which builds a DummyItemBase_t - and calls vtable slot 0x34 on the result, a name getter that falls back to stat 446, nametemplate. So it names the weapon or the nano the damage came from. It is zero in all 447 captured copies because nothing in them dealt damage through an item the client had to name.
