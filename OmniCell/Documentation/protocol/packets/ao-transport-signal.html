Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: Every byte is identified, and there are only two fields to identify. This is one of the messages the old IIR_t-only class scan could not see: our id hashes to AOTransportSignalIIR_c, vtable 0x10171EF8, reader 0x1012FAAB and writer 0x1012FA7E. The reader takes an int32 and then the rest of the stream whole - it asks the stream for its size, subtracts where it has got to, copies that many bytes into a BinaryStream member and seeks past them - and the writer does the same in reverse, emitting the int32 and then the body's bytes with no length in front of them. So the body carries no count: it is whatever is left in the packet. The reader treats a packet that leaves nothing as a bad one and abandons the message, which is why the serializer here does too. The int32 is a signal number and the body belongs to whoever subscribed to it. The dispatcher at 0x1012FBC1 hands both to 0x1012FDAA, which looks the number up in a registry and then, for each subscriber under it, rewinds the body to the start and lets the subscriber parse it - so two subscribers under one number read the same bytes twice, each in its own way. Nothing in that registry is static, so the numbers cannot be listed out of the executable; they are put there at run time. That is why this counts as complete rather than as a message with an unexplained tail. The packet is an envelope and the envelope is fully described: a number, and a body whose length is implicit and whose shape the number decides. It is complete in the way a length-prefixed string is complete without anyone knowing the text. No capture contains one, so the layout is the client's word rather than the wire's - but it is a short reader and its writer agrees with it field for field. AoTransportSignalMatchesExtractedClientLayoutByteForByte builds one from scratch and checks that eight bytes of body go out with nothing saying eight.
