Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: A container and everything in it. Five of its fields were Unknown and all five are named now, and one bit of one of them is all that is left. The client calls the container NewInventory_t - the vtable at 0x10159B44 says so - and builds it in the reader at 0x100A0859 out of the first two fields. The second one is what may be done with it, and the client tests it a bit at a time: 0x1004C0E5 tests bit 1 and prints Inv_DstCantAdd when it is missing, 0x1004BFCE tests bit 2 and prints Inv_SrcCantRemove. Everything the client builds for itself is 3; the overflow window at 0x1004B5F1 is 2, and so is every captured corpse - a corpse can be emptied and cannot be filled. The dispatcher at 0x100A0957 resolves the bag identity, and when it is a Chest_t it copies those bits onto the chest's own inventory, stores the slot number at the chest's +0x1DC, puts the chest in the character's inventory through 0x1004B4B5, and then - only if the last field is set - clears bit 0x40 of the chest's +0x4C and calls its vtable +0xAC, which sets two flags and reads stat 435, ReadOnly. That is the container window opening, and it is 1 in all fifty nine captured copies. The entries are the client's own InventoryEntry_t, which Interfaces.dll names in half a dozen exported signatures; the reader at 0x1002A20B takes two sixteen bit fields, an identity and GameData's ACGItem_t. The second sixteen bit field is the stack count - 0x1002AA8A takes it as an argument when the client puts an item in a slot, and the setter at 0x1002AF06 clamps it to 0xFFFF and marks the entry dirty when it changes. The first is flags, and every bit but one is accounted for: 1, 2 and 4 are what the slot holds and only ever one of them is set, with 0x1002A961 answering 1, 2 or 4 and 0 for an empty slot; 1 also gates reading the item at 0x1002ABD0 and 2 gates taking the entry out at 0x1002AD81; 0x20 decides whether the entry is listed, since CorpseEntry_t's builder at 0x10125D9D lists an occupied slot only when 0x1002AEA1 agrees, which it does on this bit or on the item's own stat 30 carrying 0x40; and 0x40 is set and read against equipment slot numbers. 0x80 is the one left. It has a getter at 0x1002A8C5 and a setter at 0x1002A88D and nothing in this build calls either, yet the server sets it on nine of the eleven captured entries - a bit with nowhere to lead, which is what keeps this packet amber. The three numbers after the identity are ACGItem_t's two templates and its level, which SetLevel clamps to 0x1FF, and the fourth int is the one ACGItem_t serializes and never uses: its own writer at GameData 0x1000E9A7 emits a literal zero and the reader at 0x1000E9D7 drops it.
