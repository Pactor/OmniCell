Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: Another the old IIR_t-only scan could not see: AppearanceUpdateIIR_c, vtable 0x10161D04, reader 0x10071E25. The reader is four fields and nothing conditional - a cloth list, a mesh list, an int16 and a byte - and the cloth list is not Gamecode's own: it is read by GameData.dll's operator>> for vector<ClothData_t>. The int16 is stat 673, visualflags: the dispatcher at 0x10071C07 writes it straight into stat 0x2A1 and then acts on bit 5 of it itself, looking up inventory slots 61 and 63 - the two weapon hands - and showing or hiding what is in them on the inverse of that bit. So bit 5 set means the weapons are put away. The byte is VisibleTitle, the same field SimpleCharFullUpdate carries under that name, and they are the same byte in the client. Both land on dynel + 0x208 through the setter at 0x10057800, which raises the redraw flag at + 0x2C4 when the value changes; and the other caller of that setter, at 0x100785B1, takes it from + 0x32E of a character record immediately after taking stat 673 from + 0x32C - the same two fields, in the same order, two bytes apart, exactly as they sit on the wire here. A cloth entry's first int32 is two packed shorts, and the low one is now named: ClothData_t::GetName at GameData.dll 0x1000A5F0 is a single indexed load from the table at 0x10032364, which reads hands, body, feet, arms, legs, head. The high short is not a part at all - the writer ORs 0x10000 into it when either trailing int32 is non-zero - so it is the flag that says whether the entry is three int32s or five. The two trailing int32s took three functions and a third module to name. The apply at 0x100483E5 indexes the character's appearance table with the entry's group times five plus its place and writes the texture id and this pair into three consecutive int32s of one slot; the redraw at 0x1004BBAE copies the same three back out in the same order into a GameData TextureData_t and hands the lot to VisualCATMesh_t::SetCATTextures. That one is in DisplaySystem.dll, and its loop at 0x10070247 is short enough to read whole: it draws the first of the three at the layer its caller named - 2, for clothes - with the third as the alpha mode, and then draws the second at layer 3 with no alpha at all. So the pair is a second texture over the first and the blend mode of the first, and the clear path beside it clears layers 2 and 3 together, which is the same two. TextureData_t's own reader narrows the mode further: at GameData.dll 0x1000C0B4 it turns whatever it finds into 5 or 0, so 5 is the only non-zero value the client ever holds. All 108 cloth entries in the 54 captured copies carry the short form, so none of this came from the captures.
