Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: A deposit to the market: up to eight items and an amount of credits, and the amount is what kept this amber until 2026-09-11. Extracted-client MarketSendIIR_c has vtable 0x10159BCC, reader 0x1002D83D, writer 0x1002D65A and dispatcher 0x1002D5BA. The reader clears its vector, takes an Identity, an int32 and then an X3F1-counted list of Identities, and trims that list to eight entries afterwards at 0x1002D888 - so eight is the most that can arrive whatever the count says. The writer emits the same three in the same order. The constructor at 0x1002D787 is called from exactly one place, N3Msg_SendMarketItem(vector<Identity_t> const&, int) at 0x1001E175, and that call settles two of the three: the Identity in the body is a copy of the message's own identity, which the sender takes from GetClientControlDynel, so it is the character doing the sending; and the list is the vector of item identities the function is handed. The dispatcher agrees - it resolves that Identity to a character and raises the feedback string "Feedback_MarketItemSent" - and then emits a global signal at GlobalSignals_c + 0x288 with no arguments at all, a bare notification, so the client never reads the int32 or the list back. That is why the int32 had no name: nothing on the receiving side touches it, and the signature it arrives in carries no parameter names. The name is on the sending side, in GUI.dll. Interfaces.dll re-exports N3Msg_SendMarketItem and GUI.dll imports it and calls it from exactly one place, at 0x10009DA5, inside the handler that begins at 0x10009C40 - the window whose own strings a few hundred bytes on are "Deposit to Market", "SendButton", "ItemView_" and Views/MarketSendWindow.xml. That handler reads the text field at its + 0x78, puts it through atoi, and takes zero when the field is empty. A negative amount is refused outright: it fetches LDB text 0x6E, which the string beside the call names Feedback_MarketNoNegative, pushes it at the chat signal GlobalSignals_c + 0x184 and returns without sending anything. What is left is then clamped - N3Msg_GetSkill(0x3D, 2), and 0x3D is 61, cash, with if (amount > cash) amount = cash at 0x10009D2A - so the client will not offer more than the character has. So the int32 is an amount of credits. It is not an item's price: the guard at 0x10009D8E sends whenever there is either an item or an amount, so a deposit of pure credits goes out with an empty list, and the two are independent. No capture contains one. MarketSendCarriesCreditsWithOrWithoutItems covers both shapes, and before it the message had no C# at all.
