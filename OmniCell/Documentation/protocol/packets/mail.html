Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: Nine shapes behind an int16, and one int32 in the whole message still has no name. Extracted-client MailIIR_c has vtable 0x10167ECC, reader 0x100A35DA, writer 0x100A2FBA and dispatcher 0x100A2D91. The reader takes an int16 kind and branches on it: 0 reads an X3F1-counted list of mail records; 1, 3, 5 and 7 read nothing but an unsigned 64-bit id, through BinaryStream's operator for that type; 2 reads one mail record and appends it to the list; 4 reads an id and an int32; 6 reads three strings, an Identity, an int32 and a byte kept as a boolean; 8 reads a second int16 and then falls into the same code as 4. The five client-sent kinds are named by their senders. The constructor at 0x100A339F takes an id and a kind and is called from exactly four exported functions, each pushing its own number - N3Msg_RequestMailMessage pushes 1, N3Msg_MailTakeAll pushes 3, N3Msg_DeleteMail pushes 5 and N3Msg_ReturnMail pushes 7, all four taking an unsigned __int64, which is what makes the 64-bit field a mail id. Kind 6 is the constructor at 0x100A3206, called only from N3Msg_SendMail(string, string, string, Identity const&, int, bool). That signature carries types and no parameter names, and the six were unnamed here until the call site in GUI.dll was read: at 0x101065C5 through 0x10106627 the compose window pulls its six arguments out of a Utils.dll Message by key, in the order the call pushes them - "To", "Subject", "Text", "Item", "Cash" and "Express". The last is the Standard/Express radio button in Views/MailMessageWindow.xml, whose two values are 0 and 1. The four kinds the client receives were the dead end, because the dispatcher ends each at a global signal and nothing in Gamecode subscribes. A signal scan finds the subscribers in GUI.dll. Kind 0 emits GlobalSignals_c + 0x274 with the whole vector, connected at 0x10109EF8 to the inbox handler at 0x10109405; kind 2 emits + 0x278 with one record, connected at 0x1010862C to the message window at 0x10107B87; kind 4 emits + 0x27C with the id and the int32, connected at 0x10108647 to 0x10106EAA; kind 8 emits + 0x280 with its extra int16, the id and the int32, connected twice, at 0x10108662 and 0x10109F26. That settles the record, which the reader at 0x10125F5F lays out as an unsigned 64-bit id at +8, an int32 at +0x10, strings at +0x14 and +0x30, two int32s at +0x50 and +0x58 each stored as the low half of a 64-bit pair with the high half zeroed, an int32 at +0x60 and a byte at +1; and, when that byte is not 1, an int32 at +0x64, a GameData::ACGItem_t at +0x68, an int32 at +0x74 and a string at +0x78. The inbox window declares five columns at 0x10109C2A onward - "Icon", "From", "Subject", "Sent" and "Expires" - and MailListItem_c, named by the RTTI at GUI 0x101C292C, answers for them in the switch at 0x101041CF: column 1 from record +0x14, column 2 from record +0x30, column 3 and column 4 from the two 64-bit pairs run through the date formatter at 0x10106307, which prints "not-a-date-time", "+infinity" and "-infinity" for the sentinels. The message window fills in the rest: record +0x78 goes to the MessageText view, and record +0x64 is one signed amount that goes to the Cash view when it is positive and, negated at 0x10107CA2, to the C.O.D. view when it is negative. Record +0x68 is compared against GameData::ACGItem_t::zero to decide whether there is an attachment at all, and +0x74 rides with it into the item slot as its count. Record +0x60 is a flags word: bit 1 suppresses the attachment, bit 4 gates the Return button, bit 5 drives a ViewSelector and bit 6 replaces the body with canned text 25. What is left is record +0x10. Neither consumer reads it - not the inbox item, not the message window - and a signal scan says those two are the only subscribers there are. No capture contains one. One width correction worth carrying: the shared counted-string helper at Gamecode 0x10038AF8 takes an int16 length through BinaryStream's 0x101540D8 and refuses anything from 0x8000 up, and its writer at 0x10038AB3 clamps to 0xFFFF and emits an int16 through 0x101540D4. Every string here is two bytes of length and then the characters, with no terminator - not four, which is what this entry said when it was first written on 2026-09-11. The first captures of this message arrived on 2026-09-11, from a session that bought something from the market, and until that day this message had no C# at all - which is why its id had been sitting in the audit as an unreadable packet rather than as a gap. Six of them are now in the corpus and all six read out and round-trip byte for byte. Four shapes are covered by real bytes: an inbox listing with one record, the body of the same mail with its attachment block, and two updates. They confirm the reader's branch table exactly, and they name three fields the client could only describe. The two int32s the inbox window runs through its date formatter are a sent time and an expiry: the captured pair is 1789164293 and 1820786693, which are 2026-09-11 22:04:53 - the minute the mail was sent in that session - and 2027-09-12 22:04:53, a year and a day later. The byte that ends the shared part of the record is the record's own length switch and not a field: it is 1 in the listing, where the record stops there, and 0 in the body, where the attachment block follows. The same mail arrives both ways in the same session, which is what shows it. And kind 4 reads as a flags change. Both captured updates carry the id of the mail in the listing and an int32 that is that record's flags word with bits added - 0x5C in the record, then 0x5D and 0x5F - which is what reading a mail and taking its attachment would do. Named MailUpdate for its shape rather than that reading, which rests on two packets. Two of the record's fields were named in the model on 2026-09-11 after this entry was first written, and one of them deserves a note. The int32 at + 0x64 is called Amount, which the client supports: the message window sends it to the Cash view when it is positive and, negated at 0x10107CA2, to the C.O.D. view when it is negative, so it is one signed amount and not a count - the count is the separate int32 at + 0x74 that rides with the item. The int32 at + 0x10 is called ReservedServerMetadata, and that goes further than the evidence does: what is established is that neither the inbox item nor the message window reads it and that it is zero in every captured copy. Reserved, and metadata, are both guesses. Recorded here rather than renamed, because the name is somebody's deliberate choice and the packet stays amber on that field either way.
