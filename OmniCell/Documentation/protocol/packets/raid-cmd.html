Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: Green on 2026-09-11, and the note that used to stand here had one claim in it that was wrong. Every transmitted byte is identified and every operation the selector can carry is named, from the client's own keyword table.

RaidCmdIIR_c has vtable 0x10167EF4, reader 0x100A39F2, writer 0x100A3A3F and an intentionally empty dispatcher at 0x100A3A83 - this one only travels client to server. The reader is an int32 operation into + 0x18, an Identity into + 0x1C, and then, only when the operation is exactly 5, two further Identities into + 0x24 and + 0x2C. Nothing else, and no other value of the operation changes the length.

The shared constructor at 0x100A3A86 takes the sender Identity, the operation and three operands, and it has exactly five call sites - 0x1001849E, 0x10018538, 0x100185D4, 0x10018644 and 0x10040906. Four are the named exports: N3Msg_CreateRaid sends operation 1 with three empty operands, N3Msg_MoveRaidMember sends 4 with the member instance and destination team in the first operand, N3Msg_SetLootAccess sends 5 with all three, and N3Msg_RequestRaidLocks sends 6 with empty operands. There are no hidden client-generated variants.

The fifth call site is the .raid privileged command, and this is where the old note went wrong. It said the bridge validates the selector is at most 6, and that this proves the selector range. It does not. The compare at 0x100408E4 sits inside the move branch and bounds the second number the player typed, which goes into the first operand's second word - the destination raid team index, and a raid has six teams. The selector never passes through it at all: the bridge gets it from 0x100323CE, which looks the sub-keyword up in the std::map at 0x102E3720.

That map is what names the operations, and it is filled in one run at 0x10033A26 through 0x10033B99, each keyword followed by the number stored on its node:

  1 create
  2 list
  3 listlocal
  4 move
  5 lootaccess
  6 locks

Which agrees with all four exports and closes the three values they left unnamed. Two details fall out of it. Zero is not an operation: it is what 0x10032427 returns when the keyword is not in the map, and the bridge answers that by doing nothing. And operation 3, listlocal, never reaches the wire either - the bridge branches it at 0x10040930 to a pair of local calls instead of building a message. So the values that travel are 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6.

The Identity typing is deliberate rather than sloppy. For move and loot access some operand pairs are two numbers carried through the same eight-byte Identity serializer, not world-object identities: N3Msg_SetLootAccess at 0x10018508 masks its second argument's instance to sixteen bits for the team number and normalises its fourth argument to 0 or 1 beside it.
