Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: Every byte is identified, and the last one to be identified was the trailing int32. Extracted-client GfxTriggerIIR_t has vtable 0x1015E434, reader 0x100398D3, writer 0x10039991 and dispatcher 0x10039A44; the reader takes two int32s and then one of six shapes chosen by the first, and the writer branches the same way on the same value, so the layout has two sources. The dispatcher hands each shape to a different overload of _EffectHandler_t::CreateEffect2, and Gamecode exports all six with their signatures, which names every field: shape 1 goes to CreateEffect2(int, Vector3 const&) at 0x100D1F6D, shape 2 to CreateEffect2(int, n3Dynel_t const&, int) at 0x100D2070, shape 3 to CreateEffect2(int, Vector3 const&, Vector3 const&) at 0x100D20C8, shape 4 to CreateEffect2(int, Vector3 const&, n3Dynel_t const&) at 0x100D21D0, shape 5 to CreateEffect2(int, n3Dynel_t const&, Vector3 const&, int) at 0x100D2228 and shape 6 to CreateEffect2(int, n3Dynel_t const&, n3Dynel_t const&, int) at 0x100D2339. The first int32 of every one of those is the effect - CreateGfxControl at 0x100D0656 looks it up in the effect handler's template storage and switches on the template's kind to decide which _GfxControl_t to build - and the trailing int is only there in the three shapes that carry a dynel. What that trailing int is took following through four functions and comes out named by Funcom. CreateGfxControl passes it to the _GfxControl_t constructors, which hand it to InitDynelTemplate - exported at 0x100D2F58 as the only one of the four Init*Template overloads with an int after its target - which passes it on to the locator at 0x10106BE2, which parks it at the locator's +0x94 beside the target's Identity. The locator uses it at 0x10106720: zero is skipped entirely, and anything else goes to the lookup at 0x10105E6D, which subtracts 1000, indexes the table of nineteen strings at 0x102C63A8 and hands what it finds to RCATMesh_t::GetBoneMatrix. That table is the enumeration and its strings are the names - "Bip01 Pelvis_ac" through "Bip01 R Hand_ac" - with a fallback to GetAttractor("Attractor01_head") when a bone is missing. Two values are not bones: 3000 casts the target to a WeaponItem_t and takes its frame, 3001 asks its VisualMesh_t for its attribute matrix, and the caller leaves both alone where it combines every other value with the dynel's world matrix. So the field is where on the target the effect hangs. No capture contains one; GfxTriggerDynelToDynelMatchesTheLayoutTakenFromTheClient and GfxTriggerPointToPointMatchesTheLayoutTakenFromTheClient write two of the six shapes from scratch. Before this the message had no C# at all.
