Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: The class the scan could not see. Our id hashes to KnubotOpenChatWindowIIR_c - an IIR_c and a lowercase b - so a scan for names ending IIR_t reported this as a message the client does not carry, which it plainly does: vtable 0x101715E4, reader 0x1012834E. The leading field is an int16 and not an int32, and it belongs to the base rather than to this message. KnubotBaseIIR_c::ReadSubClass at 0x10127DE6 reads it with the int16 reader, compares it against a literal 2, and refuses to read the Identity after it on a mismatch - so every KnuBot message carries it. 2 in all 175 captured copies. The two int32s after the Identity are booleans: the reader compares each against 1 at 0x10128375 and 0x1012838B and stores a byte, so anything that is not 1 is false. What they turn on is not in this module. The dispatcher at 0x101283F3 hands them to a signal on AFCM.dll's GlobalSignals_c and calls each subscriber with as many arguments as it declares - the identity alone, the identity and one flag, or the identity and both. That was written as though the names were waiting with whoever subscribes. Nobody does. The signal is GlobalSignals_c + 0xEC, and a signal scan over every shipped image - Gamecode, GUI, Interfaces, city, Vehicle, N3, Fanatic - finds exactly one site that touches that offset, the emit itself. The arity switch in the emit helper is generic code that every signal in the client goes through, not evidence that anything is listening to this one. So the two flags are read off the wire, normalised to booleans, handed to a signal with no listeners, and that is the end of them in this build. The first is 1 in 56 of the 175 copies and the second is never set.
