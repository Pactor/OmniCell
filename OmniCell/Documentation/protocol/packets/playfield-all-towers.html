Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: Every tower in the playfield, and the whole message is the list: the reader at 0x1012AEAB hands the stream straight to the X3F1 list reader at 0x1012B192, and the dispatcher feeds the result to the tower manager and clears the pass-on flag. The client calls a record TowerProxy_t - its vtable at 0x10171994 says so - and reads it at 0x1012BB14: two identities, a position, three int32s, a float and a fourth int32. Five of those were Unknowns. The routine that builds a tower's visual, at 0x1012B7C7, names three of them by what it does with each, and all three names are Funcom's rather than ours because they are DisplaySystem.dll exports. The first int32 becomes the instance half of an Identity - the type half is the constant 0xF696B, supplied by the client - and that goes to VisualCATMesh_t::SetMesh, so it is the mesh; eight values across the 105 captured towers, which is eight models. The float goes to VisualCATMesh_t::SetScale, and its eleven values run from 1.00 to 1.12, which is what a mesh scale looks like. The second int32 is the side, and the evidence is a three way switch at 0x1012B670 that turns it into a texture name: 0 gives side_beam_neutral.png, 1 gives side_beam_clan.png, 2 gives side_beam_omni.png - exactly how the Side enum already numbers them, and the captured towers carry all three. The last int32 is named for what it is rather than what it means: 0x1012A271 copies the tower's position, this and the side into a twenty byte record, and that record is MapTowerInfo_c, which Interfaces.dll exports N3Msg_GetLandControlTowerList to return a vector of and whose stride matches GUI.dll's vector of map markers - so it and the side are what the map draws a tower with. The only test the client makes on it, at 0x1012B759, answers yes to 3, 5 and 6 and no to anything else, which is the three values with exactly two of the low three bits set, and 0x1012A095 uses that to pick which towers count towards a piece of geometry over the field. Every captured tower carries 1, 2 or 4 - one bit each. The third int32 is the animation, and it is named the same way the mesh was. 0x1012B81D hands it to 0x1004E275, which is a cache over the registry at 0x101BFCE4; on a miss the loader at 0x1004E174 builds a two word key on its stack - the constant 0xFDE97 and the value - and passes it to ResourceDatabase_t::GetBinaryStream(Identity const&), imported from DatabaseController.dll. So the registry is keyed on an Identity and this int32 is its instance half, exactly as the mesh field is the instance half of a 0xF696B. What comes back is parsed into an animation set, and the caller takes its +0x78 and hands it to VisualCATMesh_t::SetAnimation(unsigned int, int, int, anim::Layer_e, anim::Direction_e, float) - the DisplaySystem export next to the SetMesh and SetScale that named the other two. The captures agree without exception: across 1,470 tower records in every capture set there are eight distinct values here and eight distinct meshes, and the two are in one-to-one correspondence - every mesh has exactly one animation and every animation exactly one mesh. Every byte of this message is identified.
