Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: Every byte is read, every captured copy round-trips byte for byte, and the two int32s inside the playfield proxy that used to block this are named by the client. Extracted-client PlayfieldAnarchyFIIR_t vtable 0x101710B8 identifies reader 0x101258E3, and that reader does almost nothing: it calls n3PlayfieldFullUpdateIIR_t::ReadSubClass, exported from N3.dll at 0x10029C24, and then reads two int32s - so PlayfieldX and PlayfieldZ close the packet and everything else belongs to the base. The base reads an int32 version and gates on it: above 1 a PlayfieldProxy_t, above 3 a DbObject_t. Every captured copy carries 4. The proxy reader at N3.dll 0x10038402 wants a 0x61 marker - anything else prints "Invalid playfieldproxy version" - and then an Identity, two int32s and a second Identity; PlayfieldProxy_t::operator== at 0x1000C0B0 compares the first three of those and not the fourth. The DbObject is the part that was wrong until 2026-09-10. The base peeks an Identity, stops if it is empty, and otherwise hands it to DbObject_t::CreateObject and lets the object read its own body through its vtable, so the middle of this message is not a flat set of fields at all; the six flat fields that used to stand here happened to add up for an ordinary playfield, which is why twelve of the seventeen copies round-tripped by coincidence. The registration table at Gamecode.dll 0x10152100 says what gets built. Identity type 51069 is a TemplatePlayfieldGeneratorData_t, read at 0x10124DC4: a revision, a version of 1, and a run-length table of everything in the playfield that is not a character. Identity type 51103 is an ACGBuildingGeneratorData_t, read at 0x100C773E, and that is a mission - every mission is a generated playfield, which is why no capture of a static playfield ever carried one. Its fields are named by the client itself: the record dump at 0x100C746B prints them as width, height, template pf and ambient colour, and the rooms are BuildingRoomInfo_t, six bytes read at 0x100CAD28, bounded at 0x100CACFB and turned into a world position at 0x100CACBC - which is what says that two of those bytes are grid X and grid Z and one is a signed floor. The leading int32 of either object is the DbObject row revision: DbObject_t::GetDbInfo at DatabaseController.dll 0x100013BE names its three columns TYPE, INSTANCE and REVISION, and ReadBlob at 0x100012B2 reads exactly those. The proxy is the part that went green on 2026-09-11, and every one of its six int32s has a name out of the client. n3Playfield_t copies the proxy to its own +8 in the constructor at N3.dll 0x1000E09B, so the exported accessors are a map of it: GetPlayfieldProxy at 0x1000C2F3 returns +8, GetModelID at 0x1000C2F7 returns the same address - making the first Identity the model this playfield is a copy of - and GetIdentity at 0x1000ABEA returns +0x18, which is the second Identity, making it the running playfield's own id. IsBattleStation at 0x1000C5C0 reads the instance half of that one and answers yes between 0x111C and 0x1124, which are playfield numbers, so it is a playfield id and not a handle. That leaves the two int32s between them, and two independent places in the client name those. The proxy dump at Gamecode 0x1015877C prints "Pf Proxy: Model=%u:%u GS=%u SG=%u R=%u" and the push sequence at 0x10025BFD feeds it +0, +4, +8, +0xC and +0x14 in that order, so GS is the first int32 and SG the second; and the table of spell-function argument names at 0x10096200 spells a proxy out one field at a time in six consecutive ids - 0x5E DestinationProxyModelType, 0x5F DestinationProxyModelInstance, 0x60 DestinationProxyGroup, 0x61 DestinationProxySubgroup, 0x62 DestinationLocalizerType, 0x63 DestinationLocalizerInstance - the same six int32s in the same order, with the first two being the model the dump already named. GS is the group and SG the subgroup. Which of the two is which is settled independently by n3Playfield_t::GetPfWithModelAndSG at 0x1000CC55, which walks the live playfields comparing the model identity at object +8 against the value at object +0x14 - the second of the two int32s - so SG is that one. PlayfieldProxy_t::operator== at 0x1000C0B0 compares the model and these two and nothing else: the second Identity is what the lookup finds, not what it looks for. Across the captures the group is 1 in all nine Arete Landing copies and 0 in the seven Rubi-Ka ones and in the mission, so an instanced playfield is group 1; the subgroup is 0 throughout, which is what a character standing in only one copy at a time gives. PlayfieldAnarchyFMissionMatchesRetailCaptureByteForByte reproduces the captured 301-byte mission from scratch, and PlayfieldAnarchyFInstancedMatchesRetailCaptureByteForByte the 198-byte Arete Landing copy.
