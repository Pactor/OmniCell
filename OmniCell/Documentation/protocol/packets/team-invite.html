Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: An Identity, a mode, a name, and four more fields the client never looks at. Extracted-client TeamInviteIIR_t has vtable 0x10167F6C, reader 0x100A4558, writer 0x100A44C5 and dispatcher 0x100A447C. The reader takes an Identity, a byte it widens to an int, and a string; then, only when that byte is not zero, an int32, a byte and two int16s. The writer narrows the mode back to a byte and branches the same way, so one byte is what travels. The dispatcher acts on the zero case alone - it gives up at 0x100A4494 when the mode is anything else, asks the team subsystem whether an invite can be taken, and puts the invite window up with the Identity and the string. So for the form the client receives, the Identity is whoever is inviting and the string is their name; the four fields behind a non-zero mode are read into the message and never used by it. The two constructors at 0x100A45E7 and 0x100A4677 take the identity, the mode and the name, and the second takes the four extra fields as well, but nothing in the client calls either - the client neither builds nor sends this message. Note also that this is one of the two ids where the client's own class-name hash disagrees with our enum; the wire is the thing to check before trusting either. No capture contains one.
