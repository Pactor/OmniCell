Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: A member joining a team or leaving one, and until two accounts were logged in at once there was no capture of it at all. That is why the model had nine fields where the client's reader has six: it opened with a byte and an int16 the client never reads and closed with an int16 it never reads either, and with nothing on the wire to contradict them the three bytes at the front and two at the back had simply stayed. Extracted-client TeamMemberIIR_t has vtable 0x101626B0, reader 0x1007A4EA, writer 0x1007A5D0 and dispatcher 0x1007A57C, and reader and writer agree on two Identities, two int32s, an int16 and a counted name. The first Identity is the member: the dispatcher resolves it in the playfield before doing anything else. The second is the team, and it is assigned to the member rather than read - 0x10065C8B stores it when its instance half is set, and writes a null identity over three fields and clears a flag when it is not, which is what makes an empty identity a removal rather than a missing value. Every captured copy carries a TeamWindow identity there, which is the type 0x10065D17 checks for. The three numbers are now all named. The first is the raid group: the dispatcher hands it to the team's add-member routine at 0x10066361, which keeps it at the team's +0x38 when the member is the local player, and what it indexes is the array of six group pointers at +0x20 - six, because a raid is six teams. The accessors at 0x10065CE1, 0x10065CFC and 0x10066512 fall back to +0x38 when asked for a negative group and clamp to the first when that is negative too, and 0x10065D2F answers "am I in a group" with +0x38 >= 0. An ordinary team is the one that is not in a raid and sends -1, which is what all four captured copies carry. The other two are the level and the profession, and the two account capture proves both: the two members carry 10 and 46 here, and 10 and 46 are exactly stat 54 in each account's own FullCharacter; they carry 4 and 14 here, and 4 and 14 are exactly stat 60. A level 46 Keeper teaming with a level 10 Fixer. The profession is sixteen bits on the wire where the Profession enum is thirty two. The name is capped at a hundred characters from both sides: the reader refuses a length above 0x64 and the writer clamps to the same number.
