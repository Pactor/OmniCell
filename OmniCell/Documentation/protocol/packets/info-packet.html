Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: Rewritten on 2026-09-11 as the one flags-driven record the client actually has, and two misplaced fields came out of it. Before that this was an abstract class with three subclasses - character, monster and tower - chosen by matching the first byte against a list of seven whole values: 0x40, 0x41, 0x43, 0x47, 0x50, 0x54 and 0x5C. The client does no such thing. It has one reader, at Gamecode 0x10045F6C, and one structure, and it tests that byte a bit at a time, so any combination of the bits is a legal message. A value outside the list of seven selected no shape at all and the body would not have been read - which is exactly what FullCharacter did with its team block and what Mail did with its whole message, both found the same day by a capture carrying a combination nobody had caught before.

The reader in full, instruction by instruction, with the record offsets it fills:

  byte to + 0, the flags
  if flags & 0x40, a byte to + 1, else + 1 is set to zero - the version
  three bytes to + 0x32, + 0x33, + 0x34
  if the version is zero, one byte read and dropped
  a byte to + 0x35
  an int16 to + 0x30
  four int32s to + 0x20, + 0x24, + 0x28, + 0x2C
  four counted strings to + 0x48, + 0x64, + 0x80 and + 4
  if flags & 0x01: a string to + 0x9C, then if flags & 0x02 an X3F1 list through 0x1012916A to + 0xB8, then an int32 to + 0x38
  if flags & 0x04: an X3F1 list through 0x10046894 to + 0xCC
  if flags & 0x08: an int32 to + 0xD8 and a byte to + 0xDC
  if flags & 0x20: twelve int32s to + 0xE0 through + 0x10C
  three int32s to + 0x3C, + 0x40, + 0x44
  if flags & 0x10 is CLEAR: eight int32s to + 0x110 through + 0x12C

Two things in that are easy to get wrong and both were wrong here. The int32 at + 0x38 belongs to the 0x01 branch and not to the 0x02 one: the jump for bit 0 clears the whole block including it, while the jump for bit 1 skips only the list and lands on it. And 0x10 suppresses rather than selects - the test at 0x1004618C jumps past the eight int32s when the bit is set.

The two real defects. The organization id is the fourth of the four unconditional int32s, and the old model made it conditional on the organization bits, so a plain character read three int32s where the client reads four and cut the four counted strings four bytes early. The city playfield id was the mirror of it: unconditional in the model, and only inside the 0x01 branch in the client. The two errors cancelled in the byte count, which is the whole reason every captured copy still round tripped - a plain character came out level again at the end, having named the wrong bytes the whole way through the middle. It worked only while a plain character's organization id was zero and its four names were empty, which is true of all 50 captured copies of that shape and is not a property of the protocol. Two copies on disk already carry an organization id of 9988 with no organization bit set, in the monster shape, which is what a controlled pet in an organization looks like.

The rewritten reader was then walked over every copy on disk - 471 out of the captures and the six in TestData, 477 in all - and it ends exactly on the last byte of each. The audit reads 532 and round trips all of them byte for byte. Four flag combinations appear: 0x40 fifty times, 0x41 once, 0x43 once and 0x50 four hundred and eighty. Nothing has ever sent 0x08, 0x20, or a record without 0x40, so the suppression pair, the twelve int32s and the dropped byte are modelled from the reader alone - and on 2026-09-12 the reader turned out to name them. It files twenty-eight of its fields straight into the examined character's stat table, each push carrying the stat id, which settles health, life, breedhostility, clan, profession, professionlevel, titlelevel, visualprofession, invaderskilled, killedbyinvaders and alienlevel; the twelve int32s go to stats 561 to 572, the faction standings; the eight PvP int32s to 674 to 684. The inherited CellAO comment, which read bit 3 as a suppression timer and level and bit 5 as faction standings, was right on both counts - and the client says so itself, through the localised text keyed TimeUntilGasChanges that the suppression pair is formatted into. The three names go to name slots 1, 2 and 4 through SetName's own setter, the city playfield to the member N3Msg_GetCityNameForClanMember reads, the org rank to the member N3Msg_GetClanLevelString returns, the side experience to the member N3Msg_GetInfoPacketSideXpBonus returns, the land list to the one N3Msg_GetGridDestinationList returns, and the list that was called towers is an ACGItem list read by GameData's own operator.
