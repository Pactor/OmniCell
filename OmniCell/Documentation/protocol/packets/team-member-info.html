Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: A team member's health and nano, which is what the bars in the team window are. This message had never been captured - it only appears once a second character is in the team - and the model had nine fields where the client's reader has five. It opened with a byte and an int16 and closed with an int16, all three invented, and 24 bytes of body is everything the client reads: extracted-client TeamMemberInfoIIR_t has vtable 0x101626D8, reader 0x1007A745 and writer 0x1007A79D, and both agree on an identity and four int32s. The four are four stats and the dispatcher at 0x1007A7DD names them by setting them: it resolves the identity, refuses if the character carries the flag at +0x140, and then calls the character's stat setter with 221, 214, 1 and 27 - maxnanoenergy, currentnano, life and health - before refreshing the team window twice, for stats 1 and 221. It applies each maximum before the current value that belongs with it, which is why the order it reads them in and the order it uses them in are not the same. The two captured copies settle which way round that is without any argument: one member is at 713 of 713 nano and 1631 of 1631 health, and the other is a level ten alt at 253 of 253 nano and 162 of 182 health. If the pair were the other way about the current health would be above the maximum.
