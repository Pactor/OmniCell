Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: The note that stood here said the client did not add up, and it was the tooling that did not. It is resolved. Every one of the 97 captured copies now parses to the last byte against the reader the client actually uses, and the layout below is that reader rather than a reading of the values.

What went wrong is worth keeping, because it was invisible from the result. MessageIds.locate walks a class name to its registration, its factory, the constructor that factory calls, and the vtable that constructor installs. It recognised the vtable store only as mov dword ptr [reg], imm32 for eax, ecx, edx and ebx - modrm 00 to 03. n3TeleportIIR_t keeps its object in esi, modrm 06, so the right constructor was invisible; the walk gave up on it, carried on through the window, and came back with the factory of the registration next to it. That handed back vtable 0x1003D0CC and a complete, coherent class - which is n3PlayfieldFullUpdateIIR_t. Its reader takes an int32 version and three floats and compares the version against 1 and 3, which is exactly the reader this page used to describe and could not reconcile with 32 of 33 copies. It was never this message's reader. Two other things helped it hide: locate defaulted to Gamecode.dll while this class lives in N3.dll, and the module bases overlap, so 0x10029C24 disassembles to plausible code in either image.

The real class is n3TeleportIIR_t in N3.dll: string 0x1003D120, factory 0x1000AC92, constructor 0x10029DE7, vtable 0x1003E68C, reader 0x10029E7F, writer 0x10029F1B, dispatcher 0x10029F87. The constructor corroborates the shape before a byte is read - it zeroes three floats at + 0x18, zeroes three more at + 0x24 and writes 1.0 into + 0x30, which is an identity quaternion, and the wire agrees: every captured heading is a unit quaternion to four decimal places.

The reader is five typed reads and a counted tail:

  0x1000B7AE to + 0x18 - three floats, a Vector3
  0x100100AA to + 0x24 - four floats, a quaternion
  0x10038402 to + 0x34 - a playfield proxy, twenty five bytes
  0x10037D8A to + 0x4C - an Identity
  0x10037D8A to + 0x54 - an Identity
  an int32 count, refused above 0x3E8, then that many bytes

0x10037D8A is the Identity reader - two int32s, type then instance - and 0x10038402 is the playfield proxy. That one names the byte this page used to call unnamed: it reads a byte, compares it against 0x61, and on anything else raises a DataStreamException carrying the string "Invalid playfieldproxy version" at 0x10041580. So 0x61 is not a value that happens to be constant in the captures - it is the only value the client will accept. After it the proxy reads an Identity, two int32s and a second Identity, and returns.

The tail is not a field. The reader takes the count, refuses anything above 1000, then takes the stream buffer at + 0x10 plus tellg, hands that pointer and the count to BinaryStream::write on the stream held at the message's + 0x5C, and seeks the outer stream past it. It is an opaque blob, kept whole for something else to read later. Counts of 0, 4, 8 and 12 appear, which is why it looked like a field of its own.

What the 97 copies say now that they parse. The proxy version is 0x61 in all of them. The first playfield Identity has four types, not two - 0xC79C, 0xC79D, 0xC79E and 0xC7A0. The first int32 in the proxy is only ever 0 or 1, in a 51 to 46 split, which is a flag. The second is zero in 62 and otherwise looks like a dynel instance - 0xC01A028F and its neighbours, the same shape of instance the login character list carries. The second proxy Identity is type 0x9C50 in 92 and 0:0 in five. The Identity at + 0x4C is 0:0 in all 97 without exception. The one at + 0x54 carries type 100001, 100002 or 100003, or 0:0.

Still amber, and now for reasons that are about the protocol rather than about a misidentified class: the two int32s inside the proxy have no name, nor do the two trailing Identities, and nothing has yet been traced that reads the blob back out of + 0x5C. Three of the four things still open here were run down on 2026-09-11 and two of them closed. The two int32s inside the playfield proxy are named, and not from here: PlayfieldAnarchyF carries the same PlayfieldProxy_t through the same reader and had already worked them out from the client's own dump format at 0x1015877C, "Pf Proxy: Model=%u:%u GS=%u SG=%u R=%u", with the order fixed by the push sequence at 0x10025BFD and the second of the two pinned independently by GetPfWithModelAndSG. So they are group and subgroup, and the non-zero subgroups here are the first ones with a value in them - PlayfieldAnarchyF has zero in all seventeen of its copies. What did not close is now a sound negative rather than an absence of effort: the two trailing Identities and the trailer blob have exactly three references each in the whole class - zeroed by the constructor, filled by the reader, put back by the writer - and the dispatcher never passes the message or any of those members anywhere, so nothing in the client reads any of them. The usual caveat holds, that the scan sees the direct [register + displacement] form and would miss an access computed off a pointer taken into a local.
