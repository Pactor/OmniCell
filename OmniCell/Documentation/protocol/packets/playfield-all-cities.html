Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: Every byte is identified. The reader at Gamecode.dll 0x101304F8 proves the two-byte field is a count of payload BYTES and not of entries: it copies exactly that many following bytes into a second stream and seeks the input past them, without looking inside. Its writer is a bare return, so the client never sends this and the reader is the only source for the envelope. What the payload contains is decided elsewhere, and the dispatcher at 0x101304D5 says where - it hands that second stream to PlayfieldCityHolderClient_c::UpdateNewHouses(BinaryStream&, bool), exported from city.dll at 0x10016317, which reads a uint32 house count and then that many twenty five byte records: three floats of position as a TilePos_c, an int32 template, one byte, and an Identity. AddNewHouse at 0x10016151 passes the template and the Identity together to the house template lookup, which is what makes the int32 a template rather than an instance. The byte is settled by the only other place that writes the same field: SlotPlayfieldHouseDemolishStarted at 0x10016AB7 finds the house standing on a tile and sets it to 1, so it says a demolition is already under way - carried here for a client arriving after one has started. A payload length of zero means nothing follows at all, the house count included, and all twelve captured copies are that case; PlayfieldAllCitiesWithOneHouseMatchesTheLayoutTakenFromTheClient covers a populated one with bytes computed from the client rather than taken off the wire.
