Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: The client both reads this message and builds it, so the fields are its own words rather than inferences. Extracted-client DoorStatusUpdateIIR_t has vtable 0x10167B20 and reader 0x100A0330; the constructor at 0x100A02C0 fills one in from a Door_t and the dispatcher at 0x100A015A applies one to a Door_t, and the two agree field for field. The leading int32 is a version checked against the static at 0x101C2020, which holds 2. The three flags are a byte each, normalised to 0 or 1 by the reader at 0x100A0138. The first is the door's lock: the constructor takes it from vtable slot 0xFC, which is the single instruction HasFlag(0x40), and the unlock routine at 0x10085613 tests that same slot and then clears exactly bit 0x40 from the flags word, so bit 0x40 is the lock. The second is whether the door is open, and that one is not an inference at all - the dispatcher runs n3RoomMonitor_t::DoorOpened when it is set and n3RoomMonitor_t::DoorClosed when it is not, both named in the import table. The int32 between them is stat 195, accesskey, read off the door by the constructor and written back by the dispatcher. Two fields are still unnamed. The third flag is byte 0x1D5 of a Door_t, and only two things in the image touch it: this dispatcher, which sets it, and 0x1007F2AE, which sets it and then opens the door. That second one is the door's vtable slot 0xB0, and slot 0xAC beside it opens the door without setting it - so a door has two ways to be opened and this marks which was used, but nothing in Gamecode calls Door_t slot 0xB0. That was re-checked on 2026-09-11 with a displacement scan rather than by eye: the image has exactly two call sites through a + 0xB0 slot, both at 0x100707BC and 0x100707C9, and both take their result as a float and multiply the two together, so they belong to some other class entirely. Which of the two ways a door can be opened this marks is therefore not established, and cannot be from this build. The trailing Identity list is read into a vector the client never fills and the dispatcher never reads; on a message that carries a lock and an access key it invites being called a keyholder list, which is why it is not. The captures now number 296, and the 179 added on 2026-09-11 are the useful ones: fourteen of them carry Open set, so doors were being opened while they were taken, and the byte and the list stayed zero and empty through all of it. Locked and the access key are zero in every copy. So the shape is confirmed by a session in which the thing the byte is supposed to distinguish actually happened, and the values still say nothing.
