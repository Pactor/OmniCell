Native client proof: MessageProtocol.dll Message_t::CreateDataBlock creates bytes 2–15; Connection.dll Connection_t::Send at RVA 0x16F3 writes bytes 0–1, 4–7; N3.dll n3InfoItemRemote_t::Write at RVA 0x98F4 writes opcode, affected identity, and ToBePassedOn at bytes 16–28. Retail captures independently match those writes. Packet body: Every byte is identified. Extracted-client WaypointPathIIR_c has vtable 0x10155DB0, reader 0x100041CB, writer 0x100040C2 and dispatcher 0x10004074; the reader takes an X3F1 list of Vector3s through the helper at 0x10004296 - which refuses a count that is not a multiple of 0x3F1 or that asks for more than 0x7530 entries, and reads three floats per waypoint at 0x1000404E - and then one float, and the writer emits the same two. The dispatcher resolves the message's identity with GetDynel and casts it to a Vehicle_t by way of CharVehicle_t, which is the first thing worth knowing: this is a message for vehicles and not for characters on foot. It then calls virtual slot 0x64 with the list and the float, and Vehicle.dll exports the name of that slot - Vehicle_t::UseWaypointPath(vector<Vector3_t> const&, float) at 0x1000CDE1. What the float is comes out of what UseWaypointPath builds with it: the path object's constructor at 0x1000F412 keeps it at +0x14, walks the waypoints adding the total length into +0x20, and sets +0x18 to length divided by the float. The path's evaluator at 0x1000F37F is handed a time - it compares that time against +0x18 and returns the last waypoint once past it, and otherwise multiplies +0x1C by the time to get a distance along the route. So +0x18 is how long the path takes, and a length divided by the float giving a time makes the float a speed. SimpleCharFullUpdate carries a path as well and it is not this one: that one has an Identity in front, a plain int32 count rather than the X3F1 form, and no speed. No capture contains one, so WaypointPathMatchesTheLayoutTakenFromTheClient pins the layout rather than checking it against retail; before this the message had no C# at all.
